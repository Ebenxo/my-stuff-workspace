What we fix, and what we hand to someone else

Scope below is the sample firm’s policy. A real firm would confirm each line against its own licences and equipment.

We do

  • Drains: clogged sinks, tubs, showers and toilets; single-fixture cable clearing.

  • Faucets and disposals: repair or swap in the same spot.

We refer out

  • Electrical, HVAC, septic tanks and drain fields, well pumps, structural repairs: different trades.

If you call about something on the second list, we say so on the phone rather than send a truck.

Flat rate or hourly? We quote a flat price and show how it is built

Flat rate

One price for the whole job, given before work. You can compare it with another bidder. The weakness: you cannot see how the number is made, and if the job goes fast, the firm keeps the difference. Software vendors that sell price-book tools say flat rate raises the average ticket by 20–30% over hourly billing. That is a vendor’s claim, not independent data.

Hourly

You pay for time and parts. It can be cheaper for a small job. The weakness: you learn the final price after the work, and a complicated job often runs past the first estimate. Roto-Rooter’s own comparison page says as much.

What we do

We give a flat price, and the written quote shows the labour hours and rate, the parts, and the credit for your service-call fee. If the job takes less time than quoted, the price stays. If it takes longer, that is our cost. If we open the job up and find something else, we stop, write a second quote, and you decide. You are not billed for work you did not approve. Texas’s plumbing board advises that an estimate be detailed enough for you to understand the work and how payment is expected.

Water heater: repair it or replace it?

Rules of thumb from plumbing company pages (Fuse Service; CR Plumbing, Dallas–Fort Worth). They are guides, not promises.

We ask for both numbers on the same visit: the repair, and a comparable new heater installed. Two tests decide it, cost and age.

The 50% rule: if the repair costs about half or more of a comparable new heater, replace it.

How age and cost combine
Heater age Repair cost against a new unit What we usually advise
Under about 6 yearsUnder halfRepair.
6 to 10 yearsAnyDecide on the 50% rule with both prices in front of you.
Over 10 yearsAnyReplace, especially with rust, corrosion or repeat breakdowns.
Any age, water coming from the tank itselfNot relevantReplace. A leak from a fitting or valve is a different case and is often repairable.
  • Usually worth repairing: a thermostat, element or valve.
  • Typical tank life: about 8–12 years gas and 10–15 years electric; tankless about 15–20 years or more. Hard water and skipped flushing shorten it. These are typical ranges, not a warranty on your heater.
  • Tankless often needs a larger gas line and its own venting, which is why it is quoted after a visit. Gas line work is on our refer-out list.
  • Permits: we pull permits where your city requires them. Ask us when you book and we will tell you what applies to your address.

The tank-leak line is the sample firm’s own position; the research sources imply it through their rust and corrosion rules but none states it as a rule.

Emergencies: gas smell and water you cannot stop

Short, general guidance from public agencies. It is not a substitute for your utility’s instructions.

If you smell gas

  1. Leave the area immediately.
  2. From a safe distance outdoors, call your gas utility’s emergency number or 911.
  3. Do not use cell phones, lights, appliances, cars, flames or sparks near a suspected gas leak.

A plumber is not the first call. Call us afterwards for any pipe work you are told you need.

Source: Wisconsin Public Service Commission, natural gas consumer safety (another state’s regulator; also check your own gas utility’s instructions). Read the page

If water will not stop

  1. Find the main shutoff. It is typically in a basement, garage or crawlspace, under the house, or outside near the foundation. Some homes do not have one.
  2. Wheel handle (gate valve): turn clockwise until snug; it may take several turns. Lever handle (ball valve): turn it until it sits perpendicular to the pipe.
  3. Open the faucets and flush toilets to relieve pressure.
  4. Call us. Also tell your insurer if interior pipes are damaged.

The street-side or meter shutoff depends on your water provider, so ask them before you ever need it.

Source: Regional Water Providers Consortium (Portland, Oregon area utilities), emergency water shutoff. The page is written for another region; your home’s valve position and your water provider’s meter rules may differ. Read the page

Once the water is stopped, most leaks can wait for the next standard-hours appointment. Sewage coming back up, or no way to stop the water, is when an after-hours call earns its premium. Call and we will tell you which it is.

What people worry about before they call

Will you tell me I need a new water heater when a repair would do?

We put both numbers on the quote: repair, and comparable new heater installed. The rule and the age test are on this page, so you can check our advice against them. Small fixes such as a thermostat or element are usually worth repairing, and we say so. If you want a second opinion, you owe us only the service-call fee.

How do I know the price will not change once you are in my house?

The quote is written and you approve it before work starts. If we find a second problem, we stop and write a second quote. You are not billed for work you did not approve. We do not offer “starting at” prices, because the number that counts is the one on your quote.

Is the service-call fee refunded if I only want a diagnosis?

No. The fee pays for the diagnosis, and if you decline the work you keep the diagnosis. It comes off the job price if you approve the repair on the same visit, or within 14 days if we order a part.

I do not know your company. Who will be in my home?

A plumber who works for us. You are told the name on the phone, and the technician carries a card with the firm’s RMP licence number. You can check that number with the Texas plumbing board before you open the door. If someone else turns up, you can send them away and call us.

Do I need a permit, and who gets it?

It depends on your city and the job. We pull permits where your city requires them, and the permit fee appears as its own line on the quote. Ask when you book and we will tell you what applies.

Why $95 for a service call, when some companies say it is free?

Some chains list a free trip in most markets and some franchises charge it, and fee policy varies by company. We print ours on this page and credit it toward a repair you approve.
