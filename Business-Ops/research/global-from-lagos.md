### Reported by the agent (third-party or search summaries; verify before relying)
- Upwork payouts to Nigeria: local bank (naira) about $0.99; Payoneer about $2; USD wire about $50 (thenigerianfreelancer.com, updated 2026-06-19, not Upwork itself). Upwork's own current fees were not verified.
- Payoneer: about $29.95 annual fee if you receive under $6,000 in 12 months; needs government ID; name must match bank account; one bank reportedly stopped USD transactions with Payoneer.
- PayPal: receiving in Nigeria reportedly began January 2026 through a Paga wallet link. No data on limits or reliability.
- CBN 2026 FX Manual (effective 2026-06-01) reportedly lets individuals use domiciliary balances without prior CBN approval; transfers up to $10,000 a day; the bank records the purpose.
- Stripe: Nigeria reportedly not a supported country for opening an account (workaround needs a foreign company). Wise: Nigerian residents reportedly cannot get USD receiving details. Both unconfirmed from primary pages.
- Raenest, Grey, Cleva: vendor claims of low fees; licences and fees not verified.
- **Unresolved conflict:** whether foreign inflows arrive as naira or USD. A May 2026 CBN circular for remittance operators says naira; other summaries say USD is selectable. Ask your bank and read the circular.

### Practical ranking (agent's judgement, not fact)
1. Marketplace payout to a Nigerian bank or Payoneer for the first job (only route with dispute protection).
2. Payoneer USD/GBP receiving account for direct clients.
3. Nigerian bank domiciliary account (SWIFT); ask about intermediary-bank deductions.
4. Fintech receiving accounts, after checking each one's CBN status yourself.
5. PayPal via Paga only if a client insists.
6. Stripe / Wise Business need a foreign entity: skip for now.

## 3. Trust
- Fixed-price protection on Upwork depends on funded milestones and using "Submit Work" (agent read search summaries only). Funded milestones remove a buyer's main fear with an unknown freelancer.
- Identity must match across ID, payout and tax info. **Do not use a VPN to appear elsewhere** (account risk and it would be a false claim).
- Academic work reports geographic discrimination on labour platforms (Galperin and Greppi); the size of any effect for Nigeria was not verified. Do not rely on anecdotes either way.
- Hypotheses to test (not measured): hosted live portfolio; milestone or deposit payment plus a short written contract; a video call with your real name and face; client-owned domain and hosting; a low-risk first step such as a paid audit or small fixed-scope starter.
- Blocker: nothing is hosted yet, so none of this proof exists publicly.

## 4. Cold email by destination (B2B)
| Destination | Rule | Must include | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| US, CAN-SPAM | No consent needed; no B2B exemption; opt-out regime; penalties up to $53,088 per email (agent-reported figure) | Accurate header and subject, ad identification, valid postal address, opt-out honoured within 10 business days | FTC compliance guide |
| UK, PECR | Limited companies: can email without consent. **Sole traders and ordinary partnerships are treated as individuals and need consent or a soft opt-in** (director verified on the ICO page). | Valid opt-out address, no disguised identity | ICO |
| Canada, CASL | Opt-in regime; implied consent only for existing relationships and conspicuously published addresses with no no-contact statement, and only where relevant to the recipient's role; sender carries the burden of proof | Sender name, mailing address plus phone/email/website valid 60 days, unsubscribe honoured within 10 business days | ISED |
| EU | GDPR legitimate interest is commonly used for role-relevant B2B but rules differ by country; Germany generally needs consent; Irish sole traders need consent | Opt-out, sender identity, data-source transparency | secondary source; verify with the national regulator |
| Australia, Spam Act | Consent needed, but a conspicuously published work address counts for work-relevant messages | Sender identity, contact details valid 30 days, unsubscribe in 5 working days | search summaries only |
Implications: US is lowest-friction for cold email (needs a real postal address in every message). UK is safe only for limited companies, and many trades are sole traders; check Companies House. Avoid cold email to Canada and Germany. Contact forms and LinkedIn are NOT a verified loophole. Nigeria's own data-protection duties were not researched: ask a lawyer.

## 5. Channels for a newcomer with no reviews

- Marketplaces: bid only under about 40 proposals in a researched vertical; stop after 10 bids with 0 replies (H1).

- Directories: use only to find businesses with weak sites.

## 6. Nigeria-side questions for an accountant (reported facts to verify, not advice)
Reported: Nigeria Tax Act 2025 applies from January 2026; residents taxed on worldwide income; first NGN 800,000 exempt; self-assessment due 31 March; penalties for failing to register; exported services reportedly zero-rated for VAT but registration/filing may still apply; the small-company exemption reportedly excludes professional services; CAC business-name registration costs are quoted by agents who profit from it.
Questions: trading form (individual, business name, limited company); TIN and registration; foreign-currency conversion and reporting; VAT on exported services; deductible expenses; whether a web studio counts as a "professional service"; treatment of Payoneer/fintech/domiciliary income; record keeping; which bank for USD and Payoneer compatibility and wire fees; source-of-funds disclosure; Nigerian data-protection duties for prospect data; contract, invoice and refund terms for foreign clients.
