# Original recommendation (not adopted)

Decided by Claude 2026-09-21 at the owner's request ("pick a trade name that resonates and is unique"). **Recommendation only.** Nothing has been renamed, registered or bought. Reversible until the owner does one of those.

## Recommendation: **Plainwork**

**Why it resonates.** "Plain" is what the studio sells: pages that say plainly what the business does, with no invented proof. "Work" is both the craft and the job. It fits the standards already on the site (no fake reviews, no hidden terms) and it suits trades and clinics, whose customers value plain dealing. It is short, easy to spell and say in any English-speaking market, and easy to say on a phone call from Lagos.

**Suggested line under it:** "Websites that say what your business actually does." (Owner to accept or change.)

**Rejected after checking:** "Fieldnote" (several web studios already use it, including one in Brooklyn and one in the UK); "Form & Flow" (no exact studio found, but `formandflow.com` is registered and the phrase is generic enough to collide; it also sits close to many "Flow" products).
**Runner-up:** "Fieldplain" (`fieldplain.com` and `.co` both unregistered, no business found), weaker on meaning.

## What only the owner can check before committing (none of this is done)
1. **CAC name availability search** in Nigeria (and the registration fee; costs reported by agents were ₦10,000-25,000 for a business name, unverified).
2. **Trademark search** in Nigeria, the US (USPTO) and the UK (IPO) for "Plainwork" in web design and software classes. A registered domain or absent search results do not clear it.
3. **Who holds plainwork.com** (WHOIS or the registrar) and whether it is for sale, and at what price. Do not overpay; `plainwork.co` is a fine primary domain.
4. **Social handles** and the marketplace profile names (Freelancer.com, LinkedIn, Payoneer name must match your legal ID anyway).
5. **Legal name vs trading name:** your bank and Payoneer use your legal name; the studio name is a trading name. Ask the accountant.
